Sound familiar?

Most of us aren't bad with money. We just aren't writing it down.

Nobody sets out to lose track. It happens quietly, one forgettable payment at a time, and then the month is over and the balance is a surprise.

  • The 25th of the month

    You check your balance and there's noticeably less in there than you expected. You genuinely cannot account for it. Not because you did anything reckless — just because nobody wrote any of it down.

  • The food delivery reckoning

    You scroll back through the app and count. Eleven orders. You'd have guessed four. The number isn't the problem; not knowing the number was the problem.

  • “We should save for that”

    The trip, the deposit, the new laptop. You want to start putting money aside, and you realise you don't actually know how much is spare each month.

Writing it down fixes almost all of that. Not because a list is magic, but because you can’t change a number you’ve never seen. That’s the entire idea behind this app — and behind every spend tells a story.

How it goes

There's not much to it, which is the point

No linking your bank. No 20-minute onboarding. No permissions you have to think hard about.

  1. 01

    Install it and start

    No account, no email, no setup wizard asking about your financial goals. It opens, and you can add your first expense about four seconds later.

  2. 02

    Write things down as they happen

    Bought a coffee, paid the electricity bill, got your salary. A few taps each. Or say it out loud and let the app fill in the form.

  3. 03

    That's genuinely the whole habit. A minute on a Sunday, and you'll know more about your money than you did all of last year.

Your data

Your money stays your business

Most finance apps open by asking for your bank login. This one doesn't ask you for anything — and here's exactly how that works.

  • It all sits on your phone

    Your entries, budgets, and categories live in the app's own storage on your device. Nothing is uploaded as you use it, and there's no account holding a copy somewhere.

  • Nothing is watching you use it

    No advertising libraries, no analytics, no third-party trackers. We don't know how often you open it, what you spend on, or where you were when you did.

  • It stays out of the rest of your phone

    The app has no access to your location, your contacts, your messages, your call history, your camera, your photos, or your files. A budgeting app has no business with any of that.

  • Backups go to your Drive, not ours

    Turn on Google Drive backup and the file goes from your phone to your own Drive. The app asks for the narrowest Drive permission Google offers, so it only ever sees the backups it made.

One thing we should be upfront about

Those Drive backups aren’t encrypted by us. The file sits in your own private Drive folder, protected by your Google account rather than by a passphrase. Proper encryption is something we want to add — but until it’s actually built, we’re not going to say it is.

And about the future

We’d rather not promise things we can’t see round the corner. Some of what people ask us for — sharing a budget with a partner, an assistant that answers questions about your spending — would need more than staying entirely on one device.

So here’s the commitment we can actually keep: anything like that will be something you switch on, never something that happens to you. We’ll tell you plainly what it involves before you do, and the app will keep working exactly as it does now if you’d rather it didn’t change.

Free vs Premium

What's free, and what isn't

Writing down what you spend is free, and stays free. Unlimited entries, three budgets at a time, the basics of where your money went, backups so you never lose any of it, and no ads. If you never pay anything, the app still does the thing you downloaded it for.

Premium is for when you want the numbers to tell you something. Trends, forecasts, comparing this month to last, spreadsheets, alerts that watch your budgets so you don't have to — plus the rest of the themes, because they're nice and they had to sit somewhere.

Questions

The things people ask before they install

Starting with the ones that are actually stopping you.

Is it actually free, or free-until-it-isn't?

Actually free. You can track every expense, set budgets, use all eight themes, and never pay anything. There's no trial clock and no point where it locks you out of your own data.

There's a Premium tier for deeper analytics, exports, and automatic backups. Ignoring it costs you nothing.

Do I have to make an account?

No. You open the app and start. There's no email, no password, no verification code, nothing to forget.

It isn't just a convenience thing — the app is built to work entirely on your device, so there's nothing an account would be for.

Where does my data actually live?

On your phone, in the app's own storage. As things work today, your entries aren't uploaded anywhere and we hold no copy of them.

The one exception is if you switch on Google Drive backup yourself — and then the file goes to your Drive, not ours.

So how do you make money off me?

We don't. There are no ads, no analytics SDKs, and no third-party trackers anywhere in the app. It also has no access to your location, contacts, messages, camera, or files.

Some people pay for Premium. That's the entire business, and we'd like to keep it that way — it means the only person we have to keep happy is you.

Will it always work this way?

The offline, no-account setup is how the app works today and how it will keep working. But we'd rather not promise things we can't see round the corner.

People ask us for features — sharing a budget with a partner, an assistant that answers questions about your spending — that would need more than one device holding everything. If we ever build something like that, it'll be a thing you switch on, we'll explain plainly what it involves first, and the app will carry on working exactly as it does now if you'd rather it didn't change.

What happens if I lose my phone?

This is the honest trade-off of keeping everything local: if the phone is gone and you had no backup, the data is gone too.

So set up a backup. You can export to CSV or PDF whenever you like, or connect your own Google Drive and let it save copies for you. Takes a minute, saves a lot of regret.

Are the Drive backups encrypted?

No, and we'd rather tell you than let you assume. The backup is a plain file sitting in your own private Google Drive folder, protected by your Google account rather than by us.

Real passphrase encryption is something we want to add. Until it's actually built, we're not going to claim it.

Can other people on my phone see my spending?

Only if you let them. You can turn on a fingerprint or face lock, and then the app won't open without you.

Your fingerprint stays in your phone's secure chip. The app just asks Android whether it's you and gets back a yes or a no.

Will it work on the metro, on a flight, in a basement?

Yes. There's no loading spinner waiting on a connection, because nothing about the app needs one. Aeroplane mode changes nothing.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet — Moneymalist is Android-only today. We'd rather say that plainly than leave you searching the App Store for it.

What exactly is free?

Unlimited entries, three budgets running at a time, four custom categories of each kind, the basics of where your money went, backing up and restoring, daily reminders, and no ads.

Premium adds the deeper analytics, exports, alerts that watch your budgets, unlimited budgets and categories, and the rest of the themes. There's a full breakdown further up this page — we've listed the exact limits rather than being vague about them.

If I stop paying for Premium, do I lose everything?

No. Every expense, budget, and category you've created stays exactly where it is, and you can keep using the app.

You just go back to the free limits for the Premium-only bits until you upgrade again. Nothing is ever deleted.

Can I get my data out if I want to leave?

Yes, and we think you should be able to. Export the lot to CSV or PDF and take it wherever you like. It's your money and your record of it.

Who's building this?

A very small team who got tired of finance apps that wanted a bank login before they'd show a pie chart. There's an email address on this site and a real person reads it.
